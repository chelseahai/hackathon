    01 / KNOWN EQUATION
    Available waist intake
    Ew < H + Eh − W − 1
    Derived from positive back takeout. At the reference body and hip ease 4, this gives waist ease below 25 cm. The current engine separately caps waist ease at 12 cm. Neither number is a fitted safety limit.
    Hem fullness does not enter this intake equation. It does affect the side-curve and transfer calculations downstream.
    02 / DEFINE THE INTENT
    When is the side straight?
    Measure waist-to-hip and hip-to-hem portions separately, then the entire underarm-to-hem side. For each, record maximum perpendicular distance from its endpoint chord, divided by chord length.
    Proposed descriptor, not an approved threshold. Also record slope and signed curvature: low curvature alone cannot distinguish a vertical side from an outward flare.
    03 / MEASURE THE EVIDENCE
    Where does a range stop?
    Log the first failed condition and the last passing sample. Refine the interval between them. Keep design boundaries separate from polygon failures, seam-review flags and physical fitting limits.
    A small set of successful examples is not a continuous safe range.
    

    THE RANGE CONTRACT
    0–1, with context.
    After boundaries are established, normalize within the valid interval for a fixed body and the other design settings.
    u = (value − minimum) / (maximum − minimum)
    Store the actual value, normalized value, interval, fixed settings, limiting condition and range-rule version together. Disjoint valid intervals require an interval ID; a fixed-value interval cannot use this division.
    When another setting changes, preserve the actual value and re-evaluate it. Never silently clamp or reinterpret an existing 0–1 value.

    APPROVED DESIGN INTENT / V1
    Two families.
    One construction.
    Fitted and relaxed use the same princess-line system. Their boundary comes from retained waist shaping; lower volume is limited separately by its flare chord.
    75%minimum shaping retained by the fitted family
    45°maximum hip-to-hem chord angle for both families
    
  
    Method, limits and reproducibility
    Reference body: bust 84, waist 68, hip 90, back length 38 cm. Hip depth 18 cm and seam allowance 1 cm remain fixed. Ease 0–12 cm and fullness 0–80 cm use current engine guards. Length 18–80 cm is this study’s search window; 80 is not an engine maximum.
    Each accepted draft is screened for sampled stitch/cutting outlines, notch correspondence, Python/browser-source parity and seam-length convergence at 32 versus 128 spline samples. This screening does not repeat DXF audits for every point and cannot certify physical fit. Sewing review flags remain separate.
    The silhouette policy classifies fitted as retaining at least 75% of available waist shaping; relaxed retains less but remains positive. The maximum lower chord angle is 45° from vertical. These are user-approved design rules, not physical-fit evidence.
    Deviation is the largest perpendicular distance from the sampled seam to its endpoint chord, divided by chord length. Slope is outward displacement divided by downward height. Signed turn and mean signed curvature are retained in the records. No straightness threshold is used for family membership.
    Side allocation keeps the back/front ratio within the side group at 3:4 and within the princess group at 4:5. Front allocation keeps the side/princess ratios within back and front at 3:4 and 4:5 respectively. At zero fullness, allocation has no added-flare effect.
